Work in progress… I'll update this tonight


We woke up early and ate a big breakfast. We left the hotel and started riding by 8 AM. The forecast called for a chance of rain, but we weren’t sure how it would turn out, so we decided to get as many miles in as possible before the sun baked us. 

A little before 10 AM, the rain came. At first it was a slow drizzle, then it came down pretty hard. We got under a tree, and Natalie put on a raincoat, and then we just rode through it. I think riding in the rain is so much easier than riding in the hot sun for me. I’ve had a few bouts with heat exhaustion, so we’ll take a rainy ride over a sunny ride any day.

We were carrying about 25 pounds of water because the pump at Frostburg was broken. We ended up not needing all of it because of the rain - riding in the rain doesn't require one to drink as much water compared to riding in the sun.

I know this is going to sound trivial, but one of the big unlocks for this trip has been metal water bottles. They are insulated in a way that keeps the water cold. We often end our rides with ice still in our water bottles. It makes such a huge difference to drink cold water when riding. I used to struggle to force myself to drink after a few hours, but now I look forward to it. Hooray for cold water.

As we planned our day, we really didn’t know how long it was going to take us to get to the top of the eastern continental divide. We built our schedule around it taking between five and eight hours. It took five - we were at the top by 1. That meant we were a little ahead of schedule as we came down the other side and into Myersdale, where we’re staying tonight. 

There are pros and cons to having prearranged accomodations. It is nice to know we have a place to stay, but if we hadn't made our reservations a few days ago, we would’ve kept riding today and added another 10 to 20 miles. Instead we stopped riding before 3 and cleaned up.

We are staying in an Airstream trailer next to the trail. It’s a very unique stay for us. It’s the first time on this trip where our bike will not be in a garage or a dedicated bike shed. The Airstream trailer has two air conditioners, and it is very comfortable. 

Since we got here early, we were able to shower and then walk down and eat at a pizza/salad buffet. The food wasn’t amazing, but we ate a lot. I’m going to have to start tapering my food intake as we wind down this ride. Currently, I am used to being able to eat as much as I possibly can at each meal. That doesn’t work for my level of activity when I return home.

As with the detour and the Paw Paw tunnel, we had worried about what was in front of us today. And in the end, it wasn’t that bad. I think there are some important life lessons there…

Tomorrow is a little bit longer a day, about the same as Tuesday. But the trail conditions look good, and we should be slightly downhill. We only have two days left on our ride. I am already starting to get sad that it is coming to an end.
